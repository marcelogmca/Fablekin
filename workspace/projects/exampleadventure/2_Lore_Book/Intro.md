The Crooked Tankard was busy enough that nobody noticed the chair leg wobbling until someone sat on it.

A merchant yelped.

A bard missed a note.

A mug slid off a nearby table and somehow landed perfectly upright.

Nobody clapped, but several people looked impressed.

At the counter, Talion leaned forward with one elbow on the wood, trying very hard to look like the sort of adventurer who was between important quests.

His cheap sword rested against his stool.

His shield was propped beside him.

His outfit looked clean enough, assuming no one inspected the sleeves.

Talion: "I’m just saying, a real adventuring party needs a reputation."

Srava sat beside him, turning a small wooden cup between her fingers. Her cheap robe had been patched at the elbow, and her wand was tucked carefully into her belt like it might try to escape.

Srava: "We have a reputation."

Talion: "We do?"

Srava: "Yes. The barkeep asked if we were paying this time."

Laios sat on Talion’s other side, taking up more counter space than seemed physically possible. His axe leaned against his knee, his bald head shone in the tavern light, and his big brown beard looked like it had recently survived a bowl of stew.

Laios: "That’s a financial reputation. Very different."

Ingrid stood behind them rather than sitting, mostly because every stool near the counter had either been taken or looked personally untrustworthy.

Her armor caught the warm glow of the lamps, and her shield rested calmly against her leg.

Ingrid: "It is not very different."

Talion: "All great heroes owe money at some point."

Srava: "Name one."

Talion opened his mouth.

Talion closed his mouth.

Talion: "I don’t know their private lives."

Laios lifted his mug.

Laios: "Smart lad. Never assume a hero’s debts. Some of them are legendary."

The tavern keeper passed by with a tray of mugs balanced on one hand and the exhausted expression of a man who had heard every possible excuse for late payment.

Tavern Keeper: "If any of those legendary debts are yours, they become my problem by sundown."

Talion straightened.

Talion: "Good sir, you wound me."

Tavern Keeper: "Not yet."

Srava covered a small laugh with her cup.

Talion: "We are professionals."

Ingrid: "We are available."

Srava: "We are loosely organized."

Laios: "We are hungry."

Talion gave Laios a look.

Laios: "What? It’s part of the brand."

Around them, the tavern roared with noise. Dice clattered in the corner. Someone argued with a chicken near the fireplace. A group of miners sang badly but with great confidence. Near the job board, two adventurers were loudly debating whether “mysterious glowing mist” counted as weather.

Talion glanced toward the board.

Most of the notices had already been taken.

The few that remained were either badly written, badly paid, or suspiciously stained.

Talion: "There’s got to be something decent left."

Srava: "Define decent."

Talion: "Rewarding. Heroic. Maybe involving treasure."

Ingrid: "Safe."

Laios: "With lunch included."

Srava: "So we’re looking for a heroic, safe, treasure-filled lunch."

Talion: "Exactly."

Srava: "That sounds like a picnic someone lied about."

Laios leaned over to peer at the nearest remaining notice.

Laios: "This one says, ‘Wanted: brave souls to investigate cellar.’"

Talion: "That sounds classic."

Laios: "Then it says, ‘Must not be afraid of unusually large beetles.’"

Talion paused.

Talion: "How unusually large?"

Ingrid: "Large enough that they felt it needed to be mentioned."

Srava: "And written in red ink."

Laios: "Could be beetle ink."

Srava: "Please never say that again."

Talion looked at another notice.

Talion: "Here’s one. Escort needed for delivery of fragile enchanted pottery."

Ingrid: "That sounds reasonable."

Srava leaned closer.

Srava: "It also says the pottery screams when frightened."

Laios: "Pottery shouldn’t know fear."

Talion: "Neither should adventurers."

Srava: "Adventurers absolutely should know fear. Fear is why adventurers live long enough to become expensive."

The tavern keeper returned, wiping the counter with a cloth that had clearly given up years ago.

Tavern Keeper: "You lot planning to stare at my wall all evening, or are you buying something?"

Talion looked at his nearly empty coin pouch.

Srava looked at Talion’s nearly empty coin pouch.

Laios looked at the stew pot.

Ingrid looked at the tavern keeper.

Ingrid: "We are considering work."

Tavern Keeper: "Dangerous habit."

Talion gave him his most confident smile.

It was not a bad smile.

It was simply a smile that had not yet met enough consequences.

Talion: "Actually, that’s what we wanted to ask."

Srava: "Preferably something not involving screaming pottery."

Laios: "Or involving lunch."

Ingrid: "Or at least fair pay."

Talion leaned a little farther over the counter.

Talion: "Do you have any odd jobs?"
