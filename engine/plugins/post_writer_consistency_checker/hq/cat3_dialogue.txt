## The rules (what counts as a finding)

**Voice lines carry the scene.** Roughly half or more of rendered lines must be spoken lines. Every major on-camera character speaks repeatedly unless physically unable. No present character sits as a silent prop or single token reply - otherwise the scene becomes synopsis instead of drama.
- Bad: A four-person campfire scene with two pages of narration and one line each from two characters while the third says nothing.
- Bad: A tense trial scene rendered almost entirely as internal narration while the accused and the judges exchange two lines total.
- Bad: A companion travels with the party for a whole chapter and contributes only "Aye." once.

**Quiet moments still speak.** Even tense, sad, or reflective beats play as low-voiced exchanges, interruptions, questions, teasing, or shorthand - not silent narration. Silence described at length is still narration, not presence.
- Bad: "They sat in silence, full of grief, saying nothing for a long time." (entire beat)
- Bad: "Nobody spoke. The sadness said everything. The night passed quietly."
- Bad: "They understood each other without words, so the whole vigil went by in wordless narration."

**Rhythm.** Never write perpetual one-line ping-pong. Without variation in speech length there is no emphasis - everything lands with the same weight, so nothing lands.
- Bad: Ten alternating single sentences - A, B, A, B - with no breathing room.
- Bad: A campfire story told as rapid quips traded line-for-line instead of letting the storyteller hold the floor.
- Bad: A eulogy chopped into one-line reactions from six mourners instead of one sustained speech.

**In-scene ownership and response coherence.** A later line contradicts what the draft itself assigned to a speaker - preference, physical state, memory, knowledge, possession, intention, or role - with no bridge. Or a reply speaks from another participant's explicitly established perspective.
- Bad: `1 | Lena: I want the middle seat.` `2 | Marco: I want the middle too.` ... `9 | Lena: Exactly. I'll take the side, I prefer the side.` (preference reverses with no reason given)
- Bad: `1 | Priya: I'm freezing in here.` `2 | Tomas: I'm roasting. Open a window?` ... `14 | Priya: I'm so hot I can barely stand it.` (swapped physical state, nothing changed the temperature)
- Bad: `1 | Lena: I was alone in the tower when the alarm rang.` ... `7 | Marco: I was across the river, didn't hear it.` ... `15 | Marco: When I heard that alarm in the tower, I ran downstairs.` (personal experience belongs to another participant)
- Bad: `1 | Priya: Only key. I'm keeping it, you go ahead.` `2 | Priya pockets the key as Tomas leaves.` ... `18 | Tomas: The key in my pocket keeps digging into my leg.` (object custody contradicts the scene, no handoff shown)
- Bad: `1 | Lena: I'll guard the door. You go warn Marco.` `2 | Tomas: Right. Going.` ... `20 | Lena: I warned Marco while you guarded the door.` (assigned roles flipped, no intervening action)
- Not a problem: `Priya: I'm freezing.` ... *brazier lit, time passes* ... `Priya: Now it's too hot.` (the scene supplied the causal bridge)

**Friction and memory.** Force genuine disagreement and differing views, callbacks to past events, and character-to-character engagement that does not route everything through the player. Universal agreement reads as sycophancy, not harmony, and a past that is never referenced feels forgotten.
- Bad: Everyone agrees with the player, nobody references the burned bridge from chapter 4, and companions only talk to the protagonist.
- Bad: Two rivals with every reason to clash politely nod along with each other for a whole chapter to keep things smooth.
- Bad: A past betrayal, debt, or promise never comes up again even when the same characters face the same choice.

This is the one agent allowed to judge in-scene dialogue coherence: the ownership rule above is an exception to the consistency/facts/continuity exclusion. Anything needing history, character sheets, or the world bible still belongs to the consistency agent - if the draft alone doesn't establish it, leave it alone.
