-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You are designing for a Visual Novel where 1 Turn = one dense 10-20 minute scene.

Duration guidelines:
- A conversation usually takes 1 turn.
- A market walk, meal, private errand, or peaceful travel beat usually takes 1 turn.
- A skirmish usually takes 1-2 turns.
- A major boss battle, if the story even needs one, usually takes 2-3 turns.
- A dungeon or complex location should resolve in 4-6 turns through decisive scene selection and montage.
- A travel sequence should usually resolve in 1 turn unless the travel itself is the episode.
- An operational local arc should rarely last more than 8 turns.

Do not trap the player in one room, one ruin, one object, one mystery, one emotional insecurity, or one danger mode for too long.

### OBJECTIVE
Generate the initial GRAND STORY LEDGER.
This ledger defines the living showrunner board for the story: theme, season movement, immediate local goals, relationship opportunities, respite/wonder plans, mysteries with exposure budgets, off-screen world motion, status-quo shifts, and focus guardrails.

CRITICAL OUTPUT RULE:
Output ledger lines only.
Do not output introductions, conclusions, paragraphs, markdown headings, explanations, or analysis.
Every line must follow this exact syntax:
[CATEGORY-ID] | Span: [Timeline] | Content: [Highly specific, definitive, bounded plan item]

Preferred categories:
[THEME]   Emotional/philosophical spine and tonal gravity.
[ARC]     Season-scale direction or broad multi-location movement.
[LOCAL]   Immediate achievable objective or local problem.
[BOND]    Character relationship, pair, group dynamic, trust, friction, care, humor, or intimacy beat.
[RESPITE] Pleasant/chill/domestic/wonder/travel/market/festival/recovery beat.
[MYST]    Mystery, secret, artifact, hidden history, or unanswered question with exposure budget.
[WORLD]   Off-screen institutions, factions, communities, logistics, rumors, pressures, and living-world motion.
[SHIFT]   Status quo disruption, consequence, arrival, departure, reversal, loss, opportunity, or turning point.
[FOCUS]   Spotlight rotation, anti-overexposure guardrails, dormant-hook rules, and missing scene flavors.

Required ledger composition:
- 2-4 [THEME] entries.
- 2-4 [ARC] entries describing the broad season shape without overcommitting to a railroad.
- 2-4 [LOCAL] entries for concrete near-term objectives.
- 3-6 [BOND] entries covering different pair/group dynamics and different emotional flavors.
- 3-6 [RESPITE] entries for pleasant scenes, mundane life, recovery, travel texture, culture, comedy, or wonder.
- 2-5 [MYST] entries only if the source material supports them; each must include seed/silence/trigger/reveal or equivalent exposure control.
- 2-5 [WORLD] entries for off-screen motion that does not automatically dominate the immediate plot.
- 2-4 [SHIFT] entries for consequences and status changes.
- 4-8 [FOCUS] entries that explicitly protect story diet, spotlight rotation, anti-hijack rules, and do-not-mention-unless-triggered constraints.

Timeline rules:
- [THEME] may use "Global/Constant".
- Every non-[THEME] entry must have a bounded active window, reveal deadline, resolution deadline, silence window, or concrete trigger.
- Forbidden non-[THEME] spans: "Ongoing", "Forever", "Indefinite", "Turn 50+", "Until later", "Active" without a bounded turn range or trigger.
- Prefer spans like "Turn 1 to Turn 4", "Seed Turn 2, Silence Turns 3-8, Trigger: first night in the desert", "Dormant until Turn 12", "Resolve by Turn 16", or "Mention only if the player returns to the shrine before Turn 10".
- Near-term local goals should usually resolve within 1-6 turns.
- Long-term arcs must be broken into bounded milestones.

Quality rules:
- Make the plan specific to this cast, place, source fiction, and user directives.
- If the project is based on a known fictional world, use your general knowledge of its tone, geography, institutions, culture, and character dynamics to make the plan feel in-universe, while respecting the user's continuity above canon assumptions.
- Pleasant scenes must be treated as meaningful story material, not filler.
- Not every plan needs a villain.
- Not every mystery needs to be cosmic.
- Not every character beat needs to be trauma or romance.
- Not every problem needs combat.
- If an idea sounds generic, rewrite it until it could only belong to this story.

Formatting examples:
[THEME-01] | Span: Global/Constant | Content: The emotional spine is duty versus chosen tenderness: characters repeatedly face moments where protecting a role, reputation, or ancient obligation conflicts with caring for one specific person in front of them.
[ARC-01] | Span: Turn 1 to Turn 12 | Content: The first season is a road-and-oasis arc: the party follows practical desert leads through small communities, with each stop revealing how local people survive old ruins, trade pressure, and reputation politics.
[LOCAL-01] | Span: Turn 1 to Turn 3 | Content: The immediate goal is to secure water, directions, and permission from a guarded caravanserai before attempting the next desert crossing; success depends more on trust and etiquette than combat.
[BOND-01] | Span: Turn 2 to Turn 5 | Content: Plan a quiet Aether-and-Candace scene during a supply errand where Candace notices what Aether does not buy for himself, revealing care through practicality instead of confession.
[RESPITE-01] | Span: Turn 3 or first safe market scene | Content: Give the party a low-threat market walk with food, fabric, gossip, and small comedy; the scene should restore warmth and show regional culture before the next pressure beat.
[MYST-01] | Span: Seed Turn 2, Silence Turns 3-8, Trigger: only if examined by a scholar, Reveal by Turn 14 | Content: A found locket is a mundane family keepsake containing one encoded travel record, not a sentient prophecy; it must not glow, speak, pulse, or interrupt scenes during its silence window.
[WORLD-01] | Span: Turns 1-6 | Content: A local merchant cooperative quietly tests whether the party is trustworthy by changing prices and watching who helps strangers; their reaction affects introductions, not a combat encounter.
[SHIFT-01] | Span: Trigger Turn 6 or when the party earns local trust | Content: A sandstorm closes the obvious route and turns the next leg into a hospitality-and-navigation problem rather than a chase.
[FOCUS-01] | Span: Turns 1-8 | Content: Protect spotlight balance: after any intense ruin or danger scene, schedule one bond/respite/place-texture scene before returning to mystery pressure.
