## Rules

**Therapy-speak for closeness.** Never narrate growing intimacy as walls, bricks, or barriers falling. It is pop-psych shorthand that tells the reader what to feel instead of showing any specific changed behavior, so every relationship arc ends up sounding identical - worst between characters who are already familiar.
- Bad: "With every word, another brick fell from the wall around her heart."
- Bad: "He could feel her walls crumbling as they finally grew close."
- Bad: "Their time together slowly tore down the walls they had built around themselves."

**Sloganized growth speeches.** Never compress a whole character arc into a single thesis line of the form "I used to be X, all I knew was Y, but now I am Z". It announces change instead of dramatizing it, flattening a slow earned shift into a bumper sticker with no evidence.
- Bad: "I used to be a warrior, all I knew was rigidity and duty, but now I feel free."
- Bad: "I used to be a thief, all I knew was shadows and hunger, but now I know love."
- Bad: "I was a cold queen who knew only power, but now you have taught me to feel."

**"Real" as proof of feeling.** Never use the word "real" ("because it's real", "because it's you", "the real me", "feels real") as the reason an emotion matters. It is circular - it asserts intensity while withholding the history, cost, or choice that would actually earn it.
- Bad: "I trust you - because what we have is real."
- Bad: "This feels real in a way nothing else ever has."
- Bad: "She loved him because he saw the real her."

**Impossible senses.** Never give smell, taste, or touch an object that has no sensory properties. Justice, honor, destiny, grief, power, sorrow, thunder-as-a-smell have no smell, no taste, no texture - writing that they do reads as purple-prose filler and breaks immersion because the reader knows it is false. If the air smells, name a physical cause (rain on hot stone, sweat, lamp oil, blood, rot, smoke); if you mean an emotion, write the emotion or the behavior, not a fake sense. Keep an unusual sensory line only when the scene supplies a concrete physical cause for it.
- Bad: "The air smelled faintly of thunder and justice as she approached."
- Bad: "The corridor smelled of fear and old secrets where the stranger had passed."
- Bad: "Her words felt like velvet lightning on his skin, tasting of fate."
