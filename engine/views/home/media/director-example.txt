--- WRITER_BRIEF ---

## SCENE REVIEW

The party has just accepted the Thornwell watchtower job. They're still at the counter of The Crooked Tankard, evening settling in, cold air and rain threatening outside. Devlin has given them the basic facts: blue lights, ground shaking, a dead well, and the Red Boars who went missing. Now Talion has asked the natural question: "So, what's the plan?" This is the moment for the party to think aloud, reveal their personalities through their priorities, and commit as a group.

## MANDATORY ORDERS

1. **Full party participation in the plan discussion.** Every character must speak and reveal their angle:
   - **Srava** should voice skepticism about entering an unstable ruin without reconnaissance. She might suggest scrying or asking locals first. Lean into her academic caution and dry humor.
   - **Laios** should counter with practicality: they need supplies first (rope, torches, rations), and the real answers are inside the tower, not in a village rumor. He's blunt, hungry, and eager to move.
   - **Ingrid** should act as the mediator—she agrees with caution (scout the perimeter, have an exit plan) but also acknowledges they can't learn much from outside. She's the one who mentions checking the tower's structure before entering.
   - **Talion** listens and synthesizes. Let him show leadership by acknowledging each person's concern and proposing a compromise.

2. **Include one short monologue** from either Laios or Ingrid. If Laios, let him talk about a past dungeon experience—something that went wrong because they didn't prepare, or a lesson he learned the hard way. If Ingrid, let her describe a watchtower she saw collapse because no one checked the foundations. Keep it under 8-10 lines but let it breathe.

3. **Acknowledge the time.** The evening is wearing on. Someone (likely Ingrid or Devlin passing by) should note that the party should rest before first light. This plants the natural transition to departure.

4. **Weave in supply prep.** Laios or Ingrid should list what they need: rope, torches, water skins, medical supplies. This can happen mid-dialogue as natural planning.

5. **Remind the player that Talion is present** by having the others glance at him or address him directly when making their points. He's the nominal leader, but they all have strong opinions.

## NARRATIVE THREADS

- **The Red Boars' fate.** Plant a subtle worry: someone notes that mercenaries with silvered weapons don't just vanish unless something powerful stopped them. Don't resolve this mystery yet—let it hang.
- **Laios's "old rules."** Continue this thread from the prologue. He can mention one more rule (e.g., "If there's writing on the walls, don't read it aloud.") and get teased by Srava. It builds his backstory without over-explaining.
- **Party bonding through disagreement.** Mild, respectful disagreement shows they're not a hive mind. Srava and Laios can go back and forth about whether to rush in or stall. Ingrid mediates. Talion decides.
- **Weather as a factor.** The cold rain outside can be mentioned—it might make travel slower, colder, and more uncomfortable. This raises the stakes slightly and adds texture.

## MYSTERIES_NOT_TO_REVEAL

- Do NOT reveal what is inside the watchtower or what the Blue Light is.
- Do NOT reveal the Red Boars' fate.
- Do NOT explain the "Great Stirring" in detail—keep it a background mystery referenced by scholars and worried villagers.
- Do NOT have Devlin suddenly confess hidden knowledge about the tower. He's a tavern keeper, not a plot device.

## WRITING CRITIQUES

- **Avoid ping-pong dialogue.** After the monologue, let responses breathe. A character can pause, reflect, or react non-verbally before speaking. Use action beats between dialogue clusters.
- **Show, don't tell** the characters' concerns. If Srava is worried, she taps her wand. If Laios is impatient, he stands up. If Ingrid is calculating, she looks toward the door.
- **End the scene with weight.** Don't wrap it up neatly. End on a moment of quiet—the party splitting up to rest or prepare, with an unspoken tension about tomorrow. Let the last line be a thought, a look, or a simple statement that hangs in the air. Example: "No one said goodnight. They just went to their rooms, and the tower waited in the east."
- **Keep the comedy alive** even during planning. Srava can quip about the "hole-in-the-world armor" being very fashionable. Laios can grumble that "investigating" doesn't pay as well as "solving." The tone should feel like a group of friends with slightly different priorities, not a military briefing.
