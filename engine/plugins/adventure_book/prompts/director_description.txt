Adventure Book: bounded D&D-style mini-adventure resolver.

Choose this when the player already chose the broad goal, and the next stretch can become a short playable sequence with structured choices, skill checks, risk/reward tradeoffs, and consequences before normal VN input returns.

Two good modes:
- Concrete resolver: the scene already has an obstacle or dilemma, and Adventure Book resolves how the attempt plays out.
- Sprinkles-on-top interlude: the story has committed to a journey, search, infiltration, errand, repair job, town traversal, or other bounded activity, and Adventure Book can create a small incident inside that scope. No explicit obstacle is required; "they continue walking/traveling/working toward the chosen goal" can be enough when a playable incident would make the skipped stretch richer.

Good fits:
- Journey seasoning: a 5-day trip, caravan route, desert crossing, road to town, sailing leg, mountain pass. The book may invent a small contained incident: weird merchant, oasis full of pink lizards, bandit trouble, strange weather, funny cactus-juice mistake, suspicious tracks, lost supplies.
- Route and obstacle problems: left/right path, shortcut vs safe road, river crossing, weather, supplies, getting lost, ambush signs.
- Environmental hazards: ruins, traps, caves, storms, fires, locked gates, unstable bridges, strange terrain.
- Committed investigation: search the area, decode clues, question locals, follow tracks, inspect a suspicious object.
- Infiltration or escape: avoid patrols, sneak through a checkpoint, hide evidence, flee a collapsing scene.
- Social pressure with stakes: convince guards, calm a crowd, negotiate passage, decide who leads the risky approach.
- Weird local complications: cursed object, bad mushrooms, suspicious animal, haunted noise, unsettling discovery.

Seasoning rule: prefer this when the next normal input would be a boring confirmation like "continue on the road", "keep searching", "follow the route", or "travel for the next few days." Adventure Book turns that dead air into a small contained adventure.

Core rule: stay inside the already chosen scope. Adventure Book may add color, danger, comedy, discovery, or complication, but it should not replace the player's goal or seize the higher plot.

Do not choose this for pure downtime, ordinary conversation, shopping, romance/intimacy, active combat, or broad freeform town/camp choice.
