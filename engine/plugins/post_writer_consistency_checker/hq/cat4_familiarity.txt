## Rules

**Familiar people sound familiar.** Intimates never explain ordinary known facts to each other, state the obvious, or act surprised by what should be natural. Doing so makes long companions sound like strangers reading their own biographies aloud.
- Bad: "As you know, my brother, we've farmed this valley for ten years since our father died."
- Bad: "You know I take night watch every night because I cannot sleep since the war - as I have told you many times."
- Bad: A husband gasps, "You hate olives?!" after a decade of shared meals.

**Reader-new, character-known facts.** Surface them only as shorthand, teasing, callbacks, assumptions, private jokes, unfinished references, comfortable silence, or irritation at habits. Full briefing-style exposition between people who already know destroys the illusion of shared history.
- Bad: A wife gasps, "You were a sailor?!" after ten years of marriage.
- Bad: A lifelong squire asks his knight, "Wait - you were orphaned as a boy? Tell me everything!" like a stranger.
- Bad: Two old partners brief each other on their own plan step-by-step as if meeting for the first time.

**No fabricated taboo.** Never invent secrecy, embarrassment, jealousy, betrayal, or getting-caught tension around behavior that is normal inside the established relationship. Fake taboo makes the world feel moralizing and false - earn tension from timing, danger, decorum, public pressure, or emotional context instead.
- Bad: An openly married couple acts ashamed of holding hands in front of old friends, framed as scandalous.
- Bad: A veteran mentor correcting a student's stance is written as suspicious, secretive, and vaguely traitorous for no reason.
- Bad: Lifelong battle-companions sharing rations are framed as if caught in a forbidden affair.
