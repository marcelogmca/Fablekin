## Rules

**Single-trait exaggeration.** Never flatten a character into one repeating note. It turns people into catchphrase machines - every line becomes predictable and no real personality survives.
- Bad: The scholar answers every line with "Quantum flux recalibration!" even when asked about dinner.
- Bad: The mercenary turns a funeral toast into another lecture about flanking maneuvers and kill zones.
- Bad: The noblewoman is cruel to every servant in every scene with no motive, history, or pressure behind it.

**Stale traits and expressions.** Flag and vary any speech, mannerism, expression, or visible trait that has become repetitive or the "new normal." Repetition turns texture into background noise - the reader stops feeling it and starts skimming.
- Bad: Every chapter ends with someone limping for emphasis six chapters after the wound healed.
- Bad: The same shiny artifact is mentioned and brandished in every scene whether relevant or not.
- Bad: A catchphrase like "By the gods, that's just like old times!" opens dialogue for the fifth chapter straight.

**Unearned twists and exhausted events.** Never force a plot twist that is cliche, nonsensical, or disruptive, and never repeat an overused event type. Unearned shocks teach the reader that cause and effect don't matter, which breaks trust in the story.
- Bad: A lost heir / sudden betrayal / tavern brawl derails a quiet negotiation for no reason, three chapters in a row.
- Bad: A secret twin bursts in to stop the wedding just as the last three tense talks were also interrupted by a shock arrival.
- Bad: Another ambush on the road erupts from nowhere while the party is mid-conversation about supplies, with no setup or stakes.

**Identical scene endings.** Never close every scene with the same cadence. Identical closings make chapters interchangeable and kill momentum - vary the landing.
- Bad: Five chapters in a row ending on "So - what do we do now?"
- Bad: Every scene closes on a rallying cry: "Then we ride at dawn!" / "Then we fight!"
- Bad: Every scene closes on a lingering stare and an unanswered question: "But could she trust him?"

Note on scene endings: you see only the current draft, not the full chapter history. Flag an ending here only if it is itself a generic close (call to arms, closing question, lingering unanswered stare) AND the compact history suggests recent chapters ended the same way. When in doubt, leave the ending alone.
