How story memory is organized

Story memory is arranged chronologically, with each chapter represented at an appropriate level of detail. Retrieved fragments are placed alongside their source chapters so the resulting context preserves the structure of the narrative.

Fidelity follows age

Fulltext becomes summary, then synopsis as chapters recede, preserving the whole timeline at a sustainable size.

Recall returns to its slot

Auto elevation temporarily promotes an important old synopsis to summary or fulltext for the current turn.

Old arcs become one piece

Arc compression merges long consecutive runs of distant synopses while retaining their location in the story.

Slotted RAG

Retrieval
Slotted RAG searches older synopsis chapters for specific relevant fragments and injects only those fragments back into the prompt. It is useful when an old detail matters, but you do not want the whole old chapter promoted.
Inject relevant chunks from old synopsis chapters.

Dynamic Elevation

LOD
Elevation promotes the most relevant old chapters from synopsis into summary or fulltext for the current turn. The budgets below are caps, so they control how much extra context the engine is allowed to spend.

Arc Compression

Optimization
Arc compression merges long runs of distant synopsis chapters into compact story-arc capsules. This keeps ancient history present without letting many small synopsis entries crowd out the current scene.
