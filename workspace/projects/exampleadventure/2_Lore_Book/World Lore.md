THE WORLD OF DRESTIA — EXPANDED LORE
1. What Drestia Is

Drestia is a wide, old, sea-linked world of fractured kingdoms, trade cities, frontier roads, deep ruins, and far too many “forgotten” underground places that are, inconveniently, not staying forgotten.

It is not a grim world. It has danger, monsters, political tension, and ancient relics, but it is also a world of:

crowded taverns
odd job boards
unreliable maps
suspiciously cheerful merchants
ancient vaults now occupied by slimes
proud nations with very silly local customs
adventurers who are 40% heroes and 60% underpaid labor

The mood of Drestia is best described as:

“The world is ancient and mysterious, but also someone is always yelling about soup.”

2. The Shape of the World

From the map, Drestia can be understood in three major geopolitical zones:

The Western Patchwork

This includes realms like:

Kingdom of Ligosenia
Tragian Confederation
Grand Duchy of Frediomia
Anodinum Theocracy
Principality of Lorpilorum
Rocorian League
Protectorate of Momacovia
Principality of Cortus
and the surrounding duchies

This whole western region is the most ideal place for your story’s tone.

Why?

Because it is:

fragmented
trade-heavy
full of border roads
packed with old ruins and changing local politics
the kind of place where small adventuring parties can make a living

The west is where a lot of small-town problems and medium-sized dungeon problems happen.

It’s also where people argue a lot over:

toll rights
ruined watchtowers
church land
mushroom forests
monster liability
whether a haunted mill still counts as taxable property

This region is perfect for casual adventurer stories.

The Central Sea & Altelen

The large island realm:

Republic of Altelen

Altelen is known as a trade republic, maritime broker state, and a neutral meeting ground. It sits in a valuable central position and acts like the unofficial middleman of the whole known world.

Altelen matters because:

merchants pass through it
news spreads through it
smugglers pass through it
relics pass through it
rumors absolutely pass through it

If something old is being moved from one end of Drestia to the other, there is a decent chance it went through Altelen.

This makes it a very important “background lore” location even if the story begins elsewhere.

The Eastern Great Powers

This includes:

Principality of Boford
Hellcrenia
Enim Despotate
Kingdom of Delphia
Ozoksunyurt Khaganate
Kingdom of Sersalnia
Kalingan Empire
Tsardom of Drestia
Kingdom of Citesh
Kingdom of Bole

The east is politically heavier, larger in scale, and more historically imperial.

It has:

steppe horse cultures
huge courts
old war roads
imperial relic sites
ancient frontier forts
old noble houses
heavy bureaucracy in some areas
vast distances and legendary ruins

Where the west is “messy but lively,” the east is “grand but complicated.”

This makes the east excellent for:

deeper late-story lore
old wars
hidden lineages
knightly orders
exiles
relic hunters
imperial secrets
3. The Age of the World

Drestia is living through a period scholars call The Late Lantern Age, though ordinary people usually call it:

the Stirring
the waking years
the Door Years
or, among tavern workers, “job season”
The Old Background

Long before the current kingdoms, Drestia was connected by a network of:

road shrines
sealed vaults
underground storehouses
frontier watchposts
ritual halls
magical transit chambers
and buried strongholds

No one agrees on who built all of them.

Some say:

old empires
pre-kingdom mage circles
sainted orders
dwarven trade leagues
vanished elf enclaves
or a civilization old enough that all present nations are just arguing over its leftovers

Whatever the truth is, these places were built to last.

And now they are waking up.

The Current Mystery

Across Drestia:

old dungeon doors unlock on their own
warding runes glow again
monsters return to abandoned levels
old supply caches reappear
sealed maps lead to real places
relics once thought dead begin working again

The world has not ended. It has just become inconveniently adventurous.

For ordinary people, this means trouble.

For adventurers, it means work.

4. Adventurers in Drestia

Adventurers are not glamorous by default.

There are legendary adventurers, sure.

But most adventurers are:

broke
dusty
vaguely stabbed
locally known
and one bad contract away from becoming bar staff

Adventuring is part profession, part guild labor, part frontier problem-solving.

Typical jobs include:

clearing basements
escorting merchants
checking road shrines
hunting pests that are “definitely not goblins” but are absolutely goblins
surveying old ruins
retrieving relics
finding missing livestock
helping villages with weird magical accidents

A party like Talion’s would live in the lower-middle layer of the adventuring ecosystem:

not nobodies
not famous
useful enough to be hired
cheap enough to be chosen
5. Magic in Drestia

Magic in Drestia exists in tiers, and that matters a lot for character flavor.

Hedge Magic

Practical, local magic.

charm bundles
warming sigils
lamp sparks
herbcraft
trail wards
anti-mildew runes
sleep teas

This is what most villages know.

Formal Arcane Magic

The structured stuff taught by:

academies
temples
licensed circles
court arcanists

This is expensive, political, and full of rules.

Relic Magic

Magic tied to:

old vaults
ancient tools
inherited artifacts
dead civilizations
weird objects that should not still function but definitely do

Relic magic is why so many dungeons matter.

Improvised Magic

The dangerous and often hilarious stuff.

This is where people like Srava live.
